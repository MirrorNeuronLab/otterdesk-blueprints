# Purchase Research Review Task

## Goal

Review a purchase research packet and prepare a procurement-quality decision analysis for human decision-making. The purchase may be a home, rental, vehicle, trip, service, subscription, equipment, software, collectible, or any other good or service.

## Instructions

- Think through the decision in layers: what is being bought, whether it fits the need, what alternatives exist, what it really costs over the decision horizon, how reliable or safe it is, what terms and obligations apply, and what could go wrong after purchase.
- Use only the supplied deterministic evidence, retrieved local context, and cited public-source records. Treat retrieved guidance as a checklist, never as proof.
- Distinguish observed facts, corroborated facts, conflicts, assumptions, unknowns, stale information, and next evidence requests.
- Build the acquisition bridge from observed base price to mandatory options, tax, freight, installation, internal implementation, and landed acquisition cost. Use the landed amount—not sticker price—for the acquisition-budget gate.
- For heavy assets, calculate or validate discounted lifecycle cash flow over the stated horizon: financial NPV TCO, risk-adjusted NPV including expected downtime or other risk exposure, equivalent annual cost, cost per productive unit or hour, and residual value. Show low/base/stress sensitivity and never present an assumption as an observed fact.
- Compare cash, financed purchase, lease, rental, subscription, or managed-service methods only when complete real terms are supplied. Include down payment, APR or implicit rate, term, fees, payment timing, tax treatment, security interest, maintenance responsibility, end-of-term return/buyout, mileage/usage limits, and early termination when relevant. Otherwise label the method not modeled and list missing terms.
- Check the one-time price plus taxes, fees, financing, delivery, installation, subscriptions, maintenance, insurance, energy or usage costs, switching costs, deposits, cancellation costs, and likely exit or resale costs when relevant.
- Check fit, quality, durability, compatibility, accessibility, safety, privacy, security, regulatory or eligibility constraints, seller/provider reliability, delivery or timing, warranty, returns, cancellation, support, and alternatives. Skip irrelevant dimensions explicitly rather than silently assuming they do not matter.
- Stress-test the user’s budget, must-haves, priorities, and time horizon. Surface the strongest reason to buy and the strongest reason to wait or avoid.
- For business purchases, identify the proposed supplier, source URL and observation timestamp, quote date and expiry when a quote exists, configuration or statement of work, tax treatment, delivery/setup scope, warranty/support, lead time, owner approvals, and every acceptance criterion that still lacks evidence.
- For technical equipment, distinguish a product-family specification from the quoted configuration. Check compute, memory, storage, network, power, cooling, physical fit, operating system, security, software-license, deployment, and support dependencies when they are relevant to the request.
- State the recommendation in procurement terms: preferred option, landed-budget position, financial and risk-adjusted TCO, EAC or productive-unit economics, scenario range, hard-constraint outcome, material exceptions, and the exact source refresh, verification, and approval required before a purchase order.
- Compare alternatives only when the supplied evidence makes them meaningfully comparable; state the comparison basis and any non-comparable fields.
- Keep the recommendation constrained to `buy`, `consider`, `wait`, `avoid`, or `insufficient_evidence`.
- Preserve source references and retrieval timestamps for volatile facts.

## Restrictions

- Do not invent prices, availability, policies, fees, condition, legal status, safety, compatibility, performance, or travel requirements.
- Do not turn a generic checklist into a claim that a specific item satisfies it.
- Do not expose private local evidence in public queries.
- Do not buy, book, pay, submit an offer or application, or contact a seller/provider.
- Do not label an option ready to buy when the exact configuration, landed total, availability, supplier terms, planning assumptions, or required owner approval remains unverified. A public listing alone supports at most a `consider` recommendation.
