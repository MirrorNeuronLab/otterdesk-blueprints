# Confidence assessment

**Section:** 21 · Evidence and confidence  
**Specification ID:** `AR-21-06`  
**Customer question:** How strongly does the available evidence support each material claim?

## What to include

- Assign confidence at the claim level using an explicit qualitative rubric.
- Consider evidence directness, agreement, coverage, freshness, reproducibility, and unresolved contradictions.
- Explain the rating and identify what additional evidence could raise or lower it.
- Keep confidence separate from business impact, risk severity, and recommendation priority.

## Why this matters

Customers need to know which findings are ready for action and which need verification. A reasoned confidence assessment is more useful than a precise-looking probability with no calibrated basis.

## Evidence to use

Use the linked evidence records, source limitations, counterevidence, and coverage notes. Apply the shared report conventions consistently.

## Expected report output

A claim-level confidence label, concise rationale, supporting and conflicting evidence, and next verification step.

## Completion and quality checks

Do not equate model certainty with evidence confidence or average away a crucial contradiction. Use insufficient evidence when the claim cannot responsibly be assessed.

Apply the [shared report conventions](../REPORT_CONVENTIONS.md), including
claim-level traceability, confidence, scope, and explicit handling of missing
evidence. This file specifies required content; it does not assert that an
analysis or test has already been performed.
