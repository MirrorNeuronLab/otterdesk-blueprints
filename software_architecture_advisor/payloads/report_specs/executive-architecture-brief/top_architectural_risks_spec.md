# Top architectural risks

**Section:** 01 · Executive architecture brief  
**Specification ID:** `AR-01-04`  
**Customer question:** Which three to five architectural risks most deserve management attention?

## What to include

- Select three to five material risks, or fewer when the evidence does not justify more.
- For each risk, state the failure or change scenario, affected business capability, and mechanism of harm.
- Separate impact, evidence confidence, and urgency; explain the ordering.
- Name the recommended next decision, its owner or proposed owner, and any immediate containment action.

## Why this matters

An executive brief must focus attention rather than reproduce the full findings register. Scenario-based risk statements make the consequences and the required response understandable without requiring the reader to inspect every graph.

## Evidence to use

Use detailed report findings, incident evidence, dependency paths, and declared business criticality. Where probability is unavailable, use an explicit qualitative assessment rather than invented percentages.

## Expected report output

A ranked risk register with finding IDs, scenario, impact, urgency, confidence, owner status, and next action.

## Completion and quality checks

Risk severity must not be inferred from confidence or metric magnitude alone. Keep unsupported possibilities out of the top-risk list or label them as verification priorities.

Apply the [shared report conventions](../REPORT_CONVENTIONS.md), including
claim-level traceability, confidence, scope, and explicit handling of missing
evidence. This file specifies required content; it does not assert that an
analysis or test has already been performed.
