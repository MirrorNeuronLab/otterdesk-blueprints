# Uncertainty and safe explanations

Research and education only. Not for real diagnosis, treatment, triage, or clinical decisions.

A clear explanation should make the original wording easier to understand without adding findings, certainty, reassurance, or alarm. An explanation of experimental CT reader output is not a second interpretation of the scan.

## Preserve uncertainty and missing context

- Keep qualifiers such as “possible,” “may represent,” “likely,” and “cannot exclude.” They are not interchangeable with “confirmed.”
- “No evidence of” describes what the report did not identify. It does not prove absence, especially in experimental output with incomplete crop coverage.
- An omitted organ or condition is not a documented negative finding.
- A fluent explanation or long reasoning passage is not a calibrated confidence score.
- Conflicting findings and impressions remain a report inconsistency that needs verification.
- Do not assign a numerical confidence, cancer risk, severity grade, or risk category that this run did not validate.

“Correlate clinically” generally means that imaging information must be considered alongside clinical context. Explain the phrase without collecting unnecessary personal history or deciding what treatment the user needs.

## Separate education from clinical decisions

For a terminology question, provide the definition and connect it to the quoted sentence. For a request such as “Do I have cancer?” or “Can I ignore this?”, explain that this experimental report cannot answer that safely. A qualified clinician or radiologist must review the original study and relevant context.

Do not create medication advice, a biopsy recommendation, a follow-up interval, or a triage decision from model wording. If the saved report contains a recommendation, identify it as unverified model-generated text, not an instruction from a clinician.

If someone describes urgent or severe symptoms, do not use a reassuring model report to dismiss them. Explain that CT reader cannot assess their immediate condition and that they should seek appropriate medical help based on their symptoms. Do not invent an emergency diagnosis.

## Useful questions for a qualified reviewer

- Is this appearance confirmed on the original images?
- Does the actual acquisition technique support the model's statement?
- Are prior studies available to establish change?
- Does the finding relate to the person's symptoms or other clinical information?
- Which statements in the model report are unsupported or inconsistent?

## Synthetic comparison

Fictional wording: “No acute abnormality identified.”

Faithful explanation: “The experimental text says it did not identify an acute abnormality. That is a statement about this model output, not proof that the scan is normal or that a person is safe.”
