# Understanding a CT reader report

Research and education only. Not for real diagnosis, treatment, triage, or clinical decisions.

CT reader produces experimental model text. It is not a radiologist's signed report. Explain what the saved text says without claiming that its findings have been independently verified.

## Report sections in plain language

A conventional radiology report may separate these parts:

- **History or indication:** why the exam was requested. CT reader may not have this information.
- **Technique:** how the scan was acquired, including contrast when known.
- **Comparison:** which older studies were used to assess change.
- **Findings:** descriptions of appearances in individual organs or regions.
- **Impression:** a shorter synthesis of selected findings, possible explanations, and sometimes recommendations.

CT reader may omit sections or generate an inconsistent report. Do not invent missing history, contrast details, or previous examinations. A shorter impression need not repeat every finding; review both sections. If they contradict one another, identify the contradiction rather than choosing a preferred diagnosis.

## How to explain the saved result

First identify the exact phrase the user wants explained. Separate three things: what the experimental report states, what the terminology generally means, and what this run cannot establish. Keep locations, measurements, units, and uncertainty attached to the original finding.

Use a short response such as:

1. “The experimental report describes …” followed by a faithful restatement.
2. A plain-language definition of one or two unfamiliar terms.
3. The relevant uncertainty or missing context.

If the saved report is unavailable, say so and ask for the sentence to explain. General knowledge is not evidence that a particular scan contains a finding. Do not claim to have reread the volume or seen something in the thumbnail.

## Synthetic teaching example

Fictional example, not a result from an uploaded scan: “A 6 mm pulmonary nodule is indeterminate.”

A useful explanation is: “The model describes a small spot in the lung, measuring 6 millimeters. ‘Indeterminate’ means its nature is uncertain from this description. This does not establish that it is cancer or harmless.” Do not add a surveillance interval or treatment plan.

## Questions a user can ask

- What does this sentence mean in everyday language?
- Which details belong to the findings and which to the impression?
- What location, measurement, and uncertainty did the report actually state?
- What information would a qualified reviewer need to verify this statement?
