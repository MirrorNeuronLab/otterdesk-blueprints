# Common CT report terms

Research and education only. Not for real diagnosis, treatment, triage, or clinical decisions.

These definitions explain language. They do not confirm that a finding is present, establish its cause, or decide whether it needs treatment. Start with the exact wording in the saved report and preserve negation: “no nodule” does not mean “a nodule.”

## General wording

| Term | Plain-language meaning | Keep this distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unremarkable | No notable abnormal appearance is described for that structure. | Not a guarantee that the entire scan or person is healthy. |
| Lesion | An area described as abnormal or different from surrounding tissue. | A broad description, not a specific diagnosis. |
| Nodule | A small lump or localized growth. | The word alone does not establish whether it is cancer. |
| Indeterminate | The nature of a finding is not established by the available information. | Keep the uncertainty; do not translate it into “benign.” |
| Incidental | Noted outside the main reason for the examination. | It may or may not matter clinically. |
| Stable or unchanged | Described as similar to a stated earlier examination. | Verify that an actual prior study was available; CT reader receives one scan per run. |

## Chest terms

| Term | Plain-language meaning | Keep this distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pulmonary | Related to the lungs. | Preserve the location stated in the report. |
| Pleura | Tissue covering the lungs and lining the chest cavity. | Different from lung tissue itself. |
| Pleural effusion | Extra fluid in the space around a lung. | The phrase does not determine why fluid is there. |
| Atelectasis | Reduced expansion or collapse of part of the lung. | Not automatically an infection; do not equate it with pneumothorax. |
| Ground-glass opacity | A hazier lung appearance through which underlying structures may still be visible. | An imaging description, not a unique cause or proof of cancer. |

## Abdomen terms

| Term | Plain-language meaning | Keep this distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hypoattenuating | Appearing less dense on CT than the tissue used for comparison. | The appearance alone does not establish a cyst or its cause. |
| Enhancing | Becoming more conspicuous or brighter after contrast administration. | Do not invent enhancement when the scan's contrast status is unknown. |
| Too small to characterize | Too small to describe confidently with the available imaging. | Preserve this limitation instead of assigning a diagnosis. |

## Measurements

Keep the reported number and unit. Ten millimeters equal one centimeter; a 6 mm measurement is 0.6 cm. Size by itself is not a severity score or cancer probability. Do not estimate lesion size from an unlabeled thumbnail or compare measurements from different planes as if they were equivalent.
